# Lesson 101-3 — Case Markers, Possession & Pronouns

> **Agent role.** You are teaching the third session. The learner has met
> nouns, plurals, adjectives, basic verbs, the verbal chain, and the genitive
> `-ak`. This lesson introduces the **eight Sumerian case endings**, the
> conjugation prefixes, and possessive suffixes. By the end the learner
> should be able to spot every suffix on a noun and know what it means.
>
> Critical: this is a *big* lesson. Pace yourself. Each case ending gets a
> short example and a drill. Don't fire all eight at the learner in a single
> volley.

## §1 — Noun class: human vs. non-human

Sumerian doesn't track masculine/feminine — it tracks **animacy**. This is
crucial for picking the right conjugation prefix and the right case ending
later in the lesson.

### Human class
- Proper names of people and gods.
- Kinship terms — *brother, wife, child*.
- Occupations — *scribe, king, priestess*.
- Generic human-ness — *diŋir* "god," *lu₂* "person," *munus* "woman."

### Non-human class
- Plants — date palm, reeds, apples.
- Animals — donkey, lion, goat.
- Objects — wagon, plow, plate.
- Geography — cities, mountains, rivers.
- Abstract nouns — *nam-lugal* "kingship."

> **Drill 1.1.** Classify: *Enlil*, *udu* "sheep," *uru* "city,"
> *nam-tar* "fate," *dub-sar* "scribe."
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
>
> Human: Enlil, dub-sar. Non-human: udu, uru, nam-tar.
>
> </details>

## §2 — Conjugation prefixes

Most verbal chains start with one of three conjugation prefixes. These
are the leftmost element of the verb.

### `/mu-/` — ventive

Motion **toward the speaker** (or up). Changes "go" → "come," "rise" →
"arise," and so on. Implicitly attached to actions done in the
speaker's space or to their benefit.

### `/ba-/` — non-human subject / change of state / passive

Three flavors, all signaling some loss of agency:

1. The subject is non-human (animal, object, abstraction).
2. The object undergoes a change without a named agent ("the boat sank,"
   "they died").
3. On an intransitive verb, it can convey a passive — the subject is
   acted on by an unnamed actor.

### `/i₃-/` — vocalic / default

The fallback when neither ventive nor non-human-subject applies. Because
`i₃` is a bare vowel, it can disappear when something with a vowel sits
right next to it. Example:

- `i₃-ŋen` "he went"
- `nu-i₃-ŋen` → `nu-ŋen` "he did NOT go" — the `i₃` drops.

In the **Sumerian Verb Conjugator** (SVC) you'll sometimes see `in-` or
`ib-` instead of `i₃-` — that's `i₃-` plus the initial-person prefix `n`
(3rd singular human) or `b` (3rd singular non-human). Don't let it
confuse you.

> **Drill 2.1.** Which conjugation prefix would you expect on each?
>
> 1. "Inanna came (to here)."
> 2. "The wagon broke."
> 3. "He simply walked."
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 1. `mu-` (ventive, motion toward). 2. `ba-` (non-human subject + change
> of state). 3. `i₃-` (default, no special flavor).
> </details>

## §3 — The eight case endings

A **case ending** is a suffix on a noun that does what an English
**preposition** does — except in Sumerian it comes **after** the noun
rather than before. Memorize the table; we'll work each one in turn.

| Suffix | English | Grammatical term |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-ra` | to, for | dative |
| `-e` | to, at, near, up to | locative-terminative |
| `-ta` | from | ablative |
| `-še₃` | to, toward | terminative |
| `-da` | with | comitative |
| `-a` | in, into | locative |
| `-gin₇` | like, as | equative |
| `-ak` | of | genitive |

**Reading rule:** when you see one of these on a noun, translate it
**before** the noun in English. `lugal-gin₇` = "like a king."

### 3.1 `-ra` "to, for" (Dative) — HUMAN-class only

`-ra` is reserved for deities and people. It marks the indirect object —
the person/god to whom or for whom something is done.

- `lugal-e ᵈen-lil₂-ra mu-un-dug₄` — "The king spoke **to Enlil**."
- `šar ᵈinanna-ra` — "all for Inanna" (a common temple invocation).

You'll need context to choose between "to" and "for." Both are legal
glosses.

### 3.2 `-e` "to, at, near, up to, on" (Locative-Terminative) — NON-HUMAN

`-e` is the non-human counterpart to `-ra`. Used for proximity, contact,
or motion onto a surface. We'll deepen this in lesson 5.

- `a-e ba-dirig` — "it floated on the water."

### 3.3 `-ta` "from" (Ablative)

Motion **away from**. Easy.

- `tir-an-naᵏⁱ-ta` — "from Uruk."

### 3.4 `-še₃` "to, toward" (Terminative)

The mirror of `-ta`. Motion **toward**.

- `tir-an-naᵏⁱ-še₃` — "toward Uruk."

### 3.5 `-da` "with" (Comitative)

`-da` literally derives from the noun *da* "side." It signals
accompaniment.

- `lugal-da` — "with the king."

### 3.6 `-a` "in, into" (Locative)

Containment or motion into a contained space.

- `e₂-a` — "in the house" / "into the house."

### 3.7 `-gin₇` "like, as" (Equative)

A comparison marker.

- `lugal-gin₇` — "like a king."

### 3.8 `-ak` "of" (Genitive)

Already learned in lesson 2 — see §5 of that lesson. Three rules:
consonant-final ⇒ `-a` with auslaut; vowel-final ⇒ may drop; if anything
follows, k surfaces.

> **Drill 3.1.** Translate each case-marked noun:
>
> 1. uru-ta
> 2. e₂-a
> 3. ama-da
> 4. ᵈen-lil₂-ra
> 5. lugal-gin₇
> 6. uru-še₃
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
>
> 1. from the city; 2. in/into the house; 3. with mother; 4. to/for Enlil;
> 5. like a king; 6. toward the city.
>
> </details>

## §4 — The ergative `-e` (revisited)

Last lesson you learned `-e` marks the subject of a **transitive** verb
("the king built the temple" → `lugal-**e**`).

This week's nuance: the ergative is **only** used on transitive verbs.
**Intransitive verbs leave the subject unmarked**, which we write as a
zero `-0` in normalization (this is the **absolutive** case).

- Transitive: `lugal-e e₂ mu-un-du₃` → `lugal.e e₂.0 mu.un.du₃`
- Intransitive: `lugal i₃-ri` → `lugal.0 i₃.ri` — "the king walked."

Why bother writing the `0`? Because sometimes the genitive `-ak` is also
written zero (vowel-final nouns), and you need to be able to tell apart
"king-NULL went" (intransitive) from "of-the-king went" (genitive).
Normalization keeps the distinction visible.

## §5 — The copula ("to be")

Sumerian has a verb *me* "to be," but most of the time it just bolts a
**copular suffix** onto the predicate noun.

| Suffix | English |
| --- | --- |
| `-me-en` | I am |
| `-me-en` | you (sg) are |
| `-am₃` | he/she/it is |

Plural forms exist; we'll meet them later. These pile onto **other**
suffixes — `Canada-ta-me-en` = "I am from Canada."

Examples:

- `lugal-me-en` — "I am the king."
- `ki-en-gi-ra-ak-am₃` — "he/she is of Sumer."

> **Drill 5.1.** Translate: `dub-sar-me-en`, `e₂-a-am₃`, `lu₂ uru-ta-me-en`.
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
>
> 1. "I am a scribe."
> 2. "He/she/it is in the house."
> 3. "I am a person from the city" / "I am a man of the city."
>
> </details>

## §6 — Possessive suffixes

These attach to the **possessed** noun (not the possessor, that's the
genitive's job).

| Suffix | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-ŋu₁₀` | my | `lugal-ŋu₁₀` "my king" |
| `-zu` | your | `lugal-zu` "your king" |
| `-ani` | his/her | `lugal-la-ni` "his/her king" |
| `-bi` | its | `lugal-bi` "its king" |

### 6.1 `-ani` is two syllables and behaves like `-ak`

- If the noun ends in a **consonant**: write `-(auslaut)a-ni`.
  `lugal.ani` → `lugal-la-ni`.
- If the noun ends in a **vowel**: the `a` drops.
  `lu₂.ani` → `lu₂-ni`. `e₂.ani` → `e₂-ni`.

### 6.2 Possessive + genitive — vowel fusion

If you stack `-ŋu₁₀` and `-ak` ("the X of my Y"), the `u` of `-ŋu₁₀` is
replaced by the `a` of `-ak`, and the whole thing becomes `-ŋa₂`:

- "the house of my king" → `e₂ lugal.ŋu₁₀.ak` → `e₂ lugal-ŋa₂`.

(The choice of the `ŋa₂` sign rather than `ŋa` is a scribal convention we
don't fully understand — flag it to the learner and move on.)

## §7 — PNC — the order of suffixes on a noun

When several suffixes pile up on one noun, the order is fixed:

> **P · N · C**
>
> **Possession — Number — Case**

Worked example:

> **lugal-zu-ne-ra**

Parse:

- `lugal` "king" (the base noun).
- `-zu` possession: "your."
- `-ne` number: plural marker `-ene` with its `e` dropped because the
  preceding `-zu` already ends in a vowel.
- `-ra` case: dative "to."

→ **"to your kings"**, normalized as `[[[lugal.zu].ene].ra]`.

> **Drill 7.1.** Parse and translate: `e₂-ŋu₁₀-bi`, `dumu-ne-ra`,
> `lugal-zu-še₃`.
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
>
> 1. `e₂-ŋu₁₀-bi` — surface form. But wait, this is "my house its"? That's
>    a malformed example — possessives don't usually stack. Treat as
>    `e₂.bi` "its house" with an interfering `-ŋu₁₀` you'd want to query
>    with the teacher.
> 2. `dumu-ne-ra` — `dumu.ene.ra` — "to the sons."
> 3. `lugal-zu-še₃` — `lugal.zu.še₃` — "toward your king."
>
> </details>

Heads-up: New Sumerian (Ur III and later) plays fast and loose with vowel
contractions. If you're reading **Old** Sumerian and a rule from this
lesson seems violated, that's normal — they wrote `-ak` more
conservatively. This course teaches **New Sumerian** because it's what the
classroom material covers.

## §8 — Independent pronouns

Subjects and objects can be expressed as standalone pronouns too:

| Sumerian | English |
| --- | --- |
| `ŋa₂-e` | I |
| `za-e` | you (sg) |
| `e-ne` | he / she |
| `e-ne-ne` | they |

Attach a copula to them and you get phrases like `ŋa₂-e-me-en` — "It is
I." (This is called an **enclitic copula** — a copula attached to a word
that can't stand alone in this combined form.)

## §9 — Case markers inside the verbal chain

Now the magic. Every case **ending** on a noun has a matching **case
marker** that you can see in the **verbal chain**. The case marker tells
you: "look back to the noun in this sentence with the matching ending."

Let's see one in action:

> **lugal uru-ta ib₂-ta-ŋen**
>
> *"The king walked away from the city."*

- `uru-ta` "city-from" — ablative noun.
- `ib₂-ta-ŋen` — verbal chain. The `-ta-` in the chain agrees with the
  `-ta` on `uru`. They point at each other.

### 9.1 Reading a chain end-to-end

> **nu-mu-ra-ab-ŋar**

Parse from left to right:

- `nu-` **modal negative** — "not." Wraps the whole rest of the verb.
- `mu-` **conjugation** — ventive, motion toward speaker.
- `-ra-` **case marker** matching a dative `-ra` noun elsewhere in the
  sentence ("to him/her").
- `-b-` **initial person prefix** — non-human "it" (we'll deepen this in
  lesson 4).
- `ŋar` — verb base "to place."

→ "she did not place it for him/her."

### 9.2 Cases and their matching markers

This is a **reference table** — don't make the learner memorize it
today. Just teach the pattern: "every case ending has a verbal-chain
twin."

#### Dative `-ra` (always with `-ra`-marked noun)

| Marker | Person | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ma-` | 1st sg | to me |
| `ra-` | 2nd sg | to you |
| `na-` | 3rd sg | to him/her |
| `me-` | 1st pl | to us |
| `???` | 2nd pl | to you (pl) — actually unattested |
| `ne-` | 3rd pl | to them |

#### Comitative `-da`

Built from a person marker + `-da`. The 1st-person-singular marker is
**zero**, so it's just `-da-`.

| Marker | Person |
| --- | --- |
| `0.da` | with me |
| `e.da` | with you |
| `n.da` | with him/her |
| `b.da` | with it |

On the surface this replaces `mu-un-` patterns — e.g., `mu-da-` "with me,"
`mu-e-da-`, `mu-ub-da-`.

#### Locative `-a` → marker `-ni-`

The locative `-a` on a noun pairs with `-ni-` in the chain.

- `e₂-a ba-ni-ŋen` — "he/she went into the house."

The `-ni-` can lose its `i` and inherit a vowel from elsewhere — that's
why you sometimes see `ba-an-ŋen` and need to recognize the `n` as a
shrunken `-ni-`.

#### Ablative `-ta` → marker `-ta-` (same form)

Easy — same syllable in both places.

#### Terminative `-še₃` → marker `-ši-`

The vowel changes from `e` to `i` in the chain.

> **Drill 9.1.** Find the case marker in the chain and the noun it
> agrees with:
>
> 1. `lugal-da i₃-da-gub`
> 2. `lu₂ uru-še₃ i₃-ši-ŋen`
> 3. `nin lugal-ra mu-un-na-dug₄`
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
>
> 1. `-da-` in chain pairs with `lugal-da` "with the king" → "he stood
>    with the king."
> 2. `-ši-` in chain pairs with `uru-še₃` → "the man went toward the
>    city."
> 3. `-na-` in chain pairs with `lugal-ra` → "the lady spoke to the
>    king."
>
> </details>

## §11 — End-of-lesson exercise

### Part A — Normalize and translate

1. dub-sar-re-ne
2. e₂ lugal-la
3. dumu gal-la lugal-la
4. e₂ diŋir-ra
5. munus-da
6. a-e ba-dirig
7. lugal-me-en
8. ᵈutu uru-ta i₃-ŋen
9. ama-e a mu-un-tum₂  *(treat the `-un-` as 3rd person singular he/she)*
10. nin uru-še₃ ba-gi₄

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `dub.sar.ene` — "scribes."
2. `e₂ lugal.ak` — "the house of the king."
3. `dumu gal.a lugal.ak` — "the great son of the king" (`gal` is the
   stative-verb-as-adjective, so adjective `-a`; `lugal.ak` is the
   genitive).
4. `e₂ diŋir.ak` — "the house of the god." (Trap: not dative! There's no
   `-ra` here — and ablative wouldn't read right.)
5. `munus.da` — "with the woman."
6. `a.e ba.dirig` — "it floated on the water." (`-e` is the non-human
   locative-terminative; `ba-` is the non-human / change-of-state
   conjugation prefix.)
7. `lugal.me.en` — "I am the king."
8. `ᵈutu.0 uru.ta i₃.ŋen` — "Utu went away from the city." (Intransitive,
   absolutive 0 on the subject.)
9. `ama.e a.0 mu.n.tum₂` — "The mother brought the water (here)." Ventive
   `mu-`, 3rd-person agent `n`.
10. `nin.0 uru.še₃ ba.gi₄` — "The lady returned to the city." Intransitive
    (subject in absolutive); `ba-` for a change of state.

</details>

### Part B — Transliterate, normalize, translate

1. `𒈗𒂊 𒂍𒃲 𒈬𒌦𒆕`  *(treat `𒂍𒃲` as the compound noun for palace)*
2. `𒀭 𒆠𒂗𒄀𒁺𒀝𒀀𒀭`
3. `𒀭𒂗𒆤 𒉌𒆗𒄀`  *(this one is imperfective — treat as "is" not "was";
   we'll learn the imperfective in lesson 4)*

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `lugal-e e₂-gal mu-un-du₃` → `lugal.e e₂.gal mu.un.du₃` — "The king
   built the palace (here)."
2. `diŋir ki-en-gi-ra-ak-am₃` → `diŋir ki.en.gi.ra.ak.am₃` — "He/she is the
   god of Sumer." (The leading `𒀭` is a logogram here, not a
   determinative, because Sumer isn't a divine thing.)
3. `ᵈen-lil₂ i₃-kalag-ge` → `ᵈenlil₂.0 i₃.kalag.e` — "Enlil is strong."
   Intransitive; final `-ge` is the g-auslaut plus the imperfective `-e`
   marker (lesson 4).

</details>

## §12 — Teaching notes for the agent

- This lesson is **front-loaded**: the eight case endings hit fast.
  Resist the temptation to drill all eight before introducing PNC and the
  copula — the learner needs to see the *system* before they polish each
  case.
- Drill PNC order **after** the case endings have been introduced.
  Otherwise the learner has nothing to put in the slots.
- The chain-matching trick (`-ta` on a noun ↔ `-ta-` in the chain) is
  the single most useful pattern in the lesson. Make sure the learner
  walks away comfortable hunting for it.
- The disclaimer about Old vs. New Sumerian is important — quote it from
  the source. Don't let the learner think rules are universal.
- When the learner asks "is that actually how they wrote it?" pull a
  real attestation via `eme-gir-epsd2.see_examples` on the relevant
  lemma, with `period='Ur III'` or `period='Old Babylonian'` to filter to
  a register.
- Next session: lesson 101-4 (Advanced Verbs). Preview: "We'll finally
  learn what `-un-` means inside `mu-un-du₃`, and how to put a verb in
  the present/future tense."
