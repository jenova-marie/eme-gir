# Lesson 101-4 — Advanced Verbs: Person, Tense & Reduplication

> **Agent role.** You are teaching the fourth session. The learner now knows
> case endings and conjugation prefixes but hasn't fully unpacked the verbal
> chain. This lesson:
>
> 1. Finishes the person-marker system (transitive perfective + intransitive
>    + transitive imperfective).
> 2. Introduces the perfective / imperfective tense distinction (default
>    past vs. present-future).
> 3. Covers the three ways to make a verb imperfective.
> 4. Covers perfective reduplication (a separate phenomenon).
>
> By the end the learner should be able to look at a verbal chain like
> `mu-un-du₃-e` and tell you that it's transitive, imperfective, 3rd-sg
> agent, ventive — "she/he will build it."

---

## §0 — Recap from lesson 101-3

Quick check:

1. Eight case endings — `-ra`, `-e`, `-ta`, `-še₃`, `-da`, `-a`, `-gin₇`,
   `-ak`.
2. Three conjugation prefixes — `mu-` (ventive), `ba-` (non-human / change
   of state / passive), `i₃-` (default).
3. Case markers in the verbal chain agree with case endings on nouns.
4. Possession suffixes — `-ŋu₁₀`, `-zu`, `-ani`, `-bi`.
5. PNC order: Possession · Number · Case.
6. Ergative `-e` on transitive subjects; absolutive `-0` on intransitive
   subjects.

## §1 — Person markers on intransitive verbs

These are suffixes that go **after** the verb base and tell us **who is
doing it**. Memorize the chart.

The model verb is `ŋen` "to go" (perfective). Its **plural form is `re₇`**
— this is one of those verbs that changes its base entirely when the
subject is plural. The Sumerian Verb Conjugator (SVC) is the easiest
reference for these alternations.

| Marker | Person | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-en` | 1st sg (I) | `i₃-ŋen-en` | I went |
| `-en` | 2nd sg (you) | `i₃-ŋen-en` | you went |
| `-0` | 3rd sg (he/she) | `i₃-ŋen` | she went |
| `-enden` | 1st pl (we) | `i₃-re₇-en-de₃-en` | we went |
| `-enzen` | 2nd pl (you pl) | `i₃-re₇-en-ze₂-en` | you (pl) went |
| `-eš` | 3rd pl (they) | `i₃-re₇-eš` | they went |

Note that 1st-sg and 2nd-sg use the **same** `-en` ending — you have to
read context (or the rest of the sentence) to tell them apart.

> **Drill 1.1.** Identify person and number in each.
>
> 1. `i₃-gub-en` "I/you stood"
> 2. `i₃-re₇-eš` "they went"
> 3. `i₃-gub` "she stood"
> 4. `i₃-gub-en-de₃-en` "we stood"
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 1. 1st or 2nd singular; 2. 3rd plural; 3. 3rd singular; 4. 1st plural.
> </details>

## §2 — Person markers on transitive perfective verbs

NOW we finally explain what that `-un-` inside `mu-un-du₃` means. On a
**transitive perfective** verb, the agent (the actor) is marked by a
single consonant sitting **before** the verb base:

| Marker | Person | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-0-` | 1st sg | `mu-gub` (`mu.0.gub`) | I stood (it up) |
| `-e-` | 2nd sg | `mu-e-gub` (`mu.e.gub`) | you stood (it up) |
| `-n-` | 3rd sg human | `mu-un-gub` (`mu.n.gub`) | he/she stood (it up) |
| `-b-` | 3rd sg non-human | `mu-ub-gub` (`mu.b.gub`) | it stood (it up) |
| `-0-...-enden` | 1st pl | `mu-gub-be-en-de₃-en` | we stood (it up) |
| `-e-...-enzen` | 2nd pl | `mu-e-gub-en-ze₂-en` | you (pl) stood (it up) |
| `-n-...-eš` | 3rd pl | `mu-un-gub-eš` | they stood (it up) |

### 2.1 Why `-un-` and not `-n-`?

Because Sumerian had no cuneiform sign for a bare `n`, scribes wrote a
syllabic sign that *includes* an `n` (like `un`, `an`, `in`) and the
vowel just inherited whatever was nearby. In `mu-un-`, the `u` comes from
the `mu-` prefix in front, and the actual agent marker is the `n`. In
`ba-an-` the agent marker is also `n`, this time with an `a` inherited
from `ba-`. **Look at the consonant**, ignore the vowel.

### 2.2 Plural forms wrap around the verb

For 1st/2nd/3rd plural, the marker that **starts** the form (`-0-`,
`-e-`, `-n-`) is paired with a suffix that **ends** the form (`-enden`,
`-enzen`, `-eš`). The verb base sits in the middle.

> **Drill 2.1.** Parse the agent and translate (use SVC or
> `eme-gir-epsd2.find_verb_form` to verify):
>
> 1. `mu-un-du₃`
> 2. `mu-ub-naŋ` (drinking — but verb is `naŋ`, not human-acceptable here)
> 3. `mu-e-du₃-en-ze₂-en`
> 4. `mu-un-du₃-eš`
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 1. 3rd singular he/she built. 2. 3rd singular non-human "it drank"
> (probably an animal). 3. 2nd plural "you (pl) built." 4. 3rd plural
> "they built."
> </details>

## §3 — Initial person prefixes (quick mention)

A finite verb can carry up to **three** person markers — initial,
final, and the suffix system you just saw. The **initial person
prefix** is the leftmost one and sits before the dimensional markers
(`-ra-`, `-da-`, etc.). Its main effect for us is that it can absorb the
vocalic prefix `i₃-`. That's why SVC shows `in-` or `ib-` instead of
`i₃-` when an initial 3rd-sg or 3rd-non-human is involved.

| Form | Person |
| --- | --- |
| ? | me |
| e | you (sg) |
| n | him/her |
| b | it / them (non-human) |
| me | us |
| (ene) | you (pl) |
| nne | them |

Don't make the learner memorize this. Just say: "if SVC shows `in-` or
`ib-`, that's the `i₃-` prefix plus an initial person marker. You'll see
it; now you know why."

## §4 — Tense in Sumerian: perfective vs. imperfective

The default Sumerian verb is **perfective** — usually translated as
English simple past. To make a verb **imperfective** (translated as
present or future in English, depending on context), Sumerian has three
different mechanisms — and which one applies depends on the verb.

> **Important.** Sumerian doesn't really track past/present/future the way
> English does — it tracks **aspect** (completed vs. ongoing). Translate
> the *meaning*, not just the form. If "she will build" sounds right, use
> future; if "she builds" sounds right, use present.

---

## §5 — Imperfective form #1: add `-e`

The most common pattern. Stick an `-e` on the end of the verb.

| Perfective | Imperfective | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mu-un-du₃` | `mu-un-du₃-e` | she will build it |
| `mu-un-ḫul₂` | `mu-un-ḫul₂-le` | she will rejoice (l-auslaut from ḫul) |
| `i₃-la₂` | `i₃-la₂-e` | he will hang (it) |

This `-e` is the **3rd-singular** imperfective marker on a transitive
verb. The other persons add the same family of suffixes you saw on
intransitive verbs — see §7.

---

## §6 — Imperfective form #2: reduplicate the verb

For some verbs, you repeat the verbal base.

- `gi₄` "to return" → imperfective `gi₄-gi₄`.

For some verbs, **reduplication also changes the base**:

- `ŋar` "to place" → imperfective `ŋa₂-ŋa₂` (not `ŋar-ŋar`).
- `naŋ` "to drink" → imperfective `na₈-na₈`.

You can't predict these from the perfective form alone — they're
lexicalized. Use **SVC** or `eme-gir-epsd2.find_verb_form` on the lemma
to surface the correct imperfective base.

---

## §7 — Imperfective form #3: change the base entirely

Some verbs simply have a different base in the imperfective. The
classic example:

- `dug₄` "to say" (perfective) → `e` (imperfective).
- Plural marû base may differ again.

Again, SVC is your friend. There's no way to predict it from EPSD2
alone.

---

## §8 — Perfective reduplication (different beast)

Here's the trap: you can ALSO see a verb base reduplicated in the
**perfective** — but with a different meaning. It marks **plural
arguments**, not aspect.

- On an intransitive perfective: reduplication signals a **plural
  subject**.
- On a transitive perfective: reduplication signals a **plural object**.

### 8.1 How to tell perfective reduplication from imperfective reduplication

Verbs that **change their base** in imperfective reduplication
disambiguate easily:

- If you see `naŋ-naŋ` (no base change), it's **perfective**
  reduplication — plural arg.
- If you see `na₈-na₈` (base change), it's the **imperfective**.

For verbs that don't change their base, you have to look at the rest of
the chain (and use SVC).

> **Drill 8.1.** Aspect check (perfective or imperfective?):
>
> 1. `mu-un-naŋ-naŋ`
> 2. `mu-un-na₈-na₈`
> 3. `i₃-du₃-du₃`
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 1. Perfective with plural argument ("she drank multiple things").
> 2. Imperfective ("she will be drinking").
> 3. Either — verb `du₃` doesn't change its base on imperfective
> reduplication, so context decides. Check SVC for the entry.
> </details>

## §9 — Transitive imperfective: agent suffixes

Imperfective transitive verbs place the agent marker **after** the verb
(the mirror of perfective, which puts it before). The endings are almost
identical to the intransitive endings — only the 3rd singular and 3rd
plural differ.

| Marker | Person | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-en` | 1st sg | `i₃-la₂-e-en` "I will hang it" |
| `-en` | 2nd sg | `i₃-la₂-e-en` "you will hang it" |
| `-e` | 3rd sg (h/sh/it) | `i₃-la₂-e` "she will hang it" |
| `-enden` | 1st pl | `i₃-la₂-en-de₃-en` "we will hang it" |
| `-enzen` | 2nd pl | `i₃-la₂-en-ze₂-en` "you (pl) will hang it" |
| `-ene` | 3rd pl | `i₃-la₂-e-ne` "they will hang it" |

**Mnemonic — write this on the board:**

> **Perfective puts the agent BEFORE the verb. Imperfective puts the
> agent AFTER the verb.**

That single sentence is the difference between past tense and present/
future tense in Sumerian.

> **Drill 9.1.** Tense and person check:
>
> 1. `mu-un-du₃`
> 2. `mu-un-du₃-e`
> 3. `i₃-du₃-en-de₃-en`
> 4. `i₃-du₃-en`
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> 1. Perfective 3rd-sg-human "he/she built." 2. Imperfective 3rd-sg "she
> will build." 3. Imperfective 1st-plural "we will build." 4. Either
> 1st- or 2nd-sg imperfective "I/you will build (it)."
> </details>

## §11 — End-of-lesson exercise

### Part A — Normalize and translate

1. ᵈen-lil₂-e ᵈinanna-še₃ igi-ni in-ši-in-bar
2. ᵈutu lugal-ra in-na-dug₄
3. lu₂-da in-da-gub
4. lugal-e diŋir-ra e₂ mu-un-na-du₃
5. lu₂-e alan i₃-dim₂-e
6. nin uru-še₃ ba-gi₄
7. piriŋ gu₃ ib₂-dug₄
8. eden-ta uru-še₃ i₃-re₇-en-ze₂-en
9. piriŋ-piriŋ-ŋe udu i₃-gu₇-e

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `ᵈen.lil₂.e ᵈinanna.še₃ igi.ani i.ši.n.bar` — "Enlil looked at
   Inanna." Compound verb igi–bar; `igi` carries the possessive `-ani` (a
   dropped because of vowel-final `igi`); `-ši-` in the chain agrees with
   `-še₃` on Inanna.
2. `ᵈutu.e lugal.ra in.n.dug₄` — "Utu spoke to the king." Note: the
   ergative `-e` happens to be omitted on `ᵈutu` — that's a real-world
   irregularity, not a mistake. `-na-` in the chain agrees with dative
   `-ra` on the king.
3. `lu₂.da i.n.da.gub` — "He stood with the man." `-da-` in the chain
   agrees with `-da` on `lu₂`.
4. `lugal.e diŋir.ra e₂ mu.n.na.du₃` — "The king built the temple for
   the god (here)." Ventive `mu-`, 3rd-sg `n`, dative-chain `-na-`
   agrees with `diŋir-ra`.
5. `lu₂.e alan.0 i₃.dim₂.e` — "The man will fashion the statue."
   Imperfective form #1 (add `-e`); 3rd-singular.
6. `nin.0 uru.še₃ ba.gi₄` — "The lady returned to the city."
   Intransitive perfective; `ba-` for change of state.
7. `piriŋ.0 gu₃ i₃.b.dug₄` — "The lion roared." Compound verb gu₃–dug₄;
   intransitive; `b` for non-human 3rd-singular.
8. `eden.ta uru.še₃ i.re₇.enzen` — "You (pl) went from the steppe to the
   city." `re₇` is the plural base of `du`.
9. `piriŋ.piriŋ.e udu.0 i₃.gu₇.e` — "The lions eat the sheep."
   Reduplication of `piriŋ` for plural subject; imperfective form #1
   (add `-e`); 3rd-singular ending.

</details>

### Part B — Transliterate, normalize, translate

1. `𒄊 𒅗𒌈𒁉`  *(compound verb in red — the second sign picks up an
   auslaut)*
2. `𒄑𒉋𒂵𒈨𒌍 𒈗 𒌁 𒀭 𒈾 𒆠 𒀝`  *(the `𒆠` is the determinative
   "place")*
3. `𒂍 𒈬𒆕𒌦𒉈𒂗`

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `piriŋ gu₃ ib₂-be₂` → `piriŋ.0 gu₃ i₃.b.e₂` — "The lion roars!"
   Imperfective form #3 (base change): gu₃–dug₄ becomes gu₃–e in the
   imperfective. Non-human `b`; the `e` of the new base picks up the
   `b`-auslaut from `ib₂`.
2. `bil₃-ga-meš lugal tir-an-naᵏⁱ-ak` → `bil₃.ga.meš lugal
   tir.an.na^{ki}.ak` — "King Gilgamesh of Uruk" (also valid:
   "Gilgamesh, king of Uruk"). The genitive `-ak` is written
   explicitly here on a vowel-final noun — older / temple-Sumerian
   style. Note `𒀭` is the syllable *an*, NOT a divine determinative.
3. `e₂ mu-du₃-un-de₃-en` → `e₂.0 mu.0.du₃.enden` — "We built the temple
   (here)." Transitive perfective 1st-plural `-0-...-enden`. Object `e₂`
   is in absolutive (no explicit subject in the clause; the agent is
   inside the chain).

</details>

## §12 — Teaching notes for the agent

- The **single most useful** mnemonic in this lesson is: "perfective puts
  the agent BEFORE the verb base; imperfective puts the agent AFTER it."
  Drill this until the learner can recite it.
- Reach for `eme-gir-epsd2.find_verb_form` whenever a learner asks
  "which form of the verb is this?" — the tool surfaces the exact morph
  row from the corpus with an attested cited line, so the learner sees
  the actual form being used.
- The three imperfective formation rules (add `-e`, reduplicate, change
  base) cannot be predicted from the perfective alone for any single
  verb. The learner needs to **look up** each verb. Don't let them
  invent forms — make them check.
- `du` / `ŋen` is a great verb to use as a worked example because the
  perfective is `ŋen` and the plural is `re₇` — same base, totally
  different surface forms. This is also a chance to introduce SVC.
