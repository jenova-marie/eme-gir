## §0 — Recap from lesson 101-4

Quick check:

1. Perfective puts the agent **before** the verb; imperfective puts the
   agent **after** the verb.
2. Three ways to make a verb imperfective — add `-e`, reduplicate, or
   change the verbal base.
3. Reduplication signals **plural arguments** in perfective; **aspect**
   in imperfective. Verbs that change base on imperfective reduplication
   disambiguate easily.
4. Intransitive and imperfective-transitive endings are nearly identical
   — `-en, -en, -e/-0, -enden, -enzen, -ene/-eš`.

## §1 — Direct objects in the verbal chain

Up to now you've expressed the object as a separate noun ("the king built
**the temple**"). Sumerian lets you absorb the object pronoun directly
into the verbal chain.

Compare:

| Verbose | Compressed |
| --- | --- |
| `ŋa₂-e za-e-ra ki i₃-ra-aŋ₂` | `ŋa₂-e ki i₃-aŋ₂-en` |
| "I love you (with explicit pronoun)" | "I love you (you-marker on verb)" |

Where the object marker sits depends on aspect — the **mirror** of agent
placement from lesson 4.

| Person | Perfective | Imperfective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st sg (me) | `-VB-en` | `-n-VB-` |
| 2nd sg (you) | `-VB-en` | `-n-VB-` |
| 3rd sg human | `-VB-0` | `-e-VB-` |
| 3rd sg non-human | `-VB-0` | `-b-VB-` |
| 1st pl (us) | `-VB-enden` | ??? (unattested) |
| 2nd pl (you pl) | `-VB-enzen` | ??? (unattested) |
| 3rd pl (them) | `-VB-eš` | `-ne-VB-` |

### 1.1 The collision problem

If you stare at the perfective object endings, you'll notice they're
**identical** to the **intransitive** verb endings from lesson 4. To
disambiguate, you have to know whether the verb at hand is transitive or
intransitive — vocabulary, not grammar.

> **Drill 1.1.** `i₃-gub-en-de₃-en` — which is it?
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
> `gub` is intransitive ("to stand"), so the `-enden` is a **subject**
> ending: "we stood." If the verb were transitive, `-enden` could be the
> 1st-plural **object** ("they X'd us").
> </details>

## §2 — Modal prefixes

These attach **before** the conjugation prefix and change the mood of the
sentence. Three to memorize today plus one bonus.

| Modal prefix | English | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `nu-` | not | negative |
| `ga-` | let me/us | cohortative (1st-person only) |
| `ḫa-` | may he/she/it, let | precative |
| `ba-ra-` | absolutely not, certainly not | emphatic negative |

### 2.1 `nu-` — straight negation

Negates the rest of the verbal chain. Doesn't change the meaning of
anything else.

- `mu-un-du₃` "he built it" → `nu-mu-un-du₃` "he did **not** build it."

`nu-` flips to `la-` or `li-` when followed by `ba-` or `bi₂-`. (We
haven't covered `bi₂-` formally — flag this and move on.)

`nu-` is also the only modal that can stand on its own as a generic "no."

### 2.2 `ga-` — cohortative ("let me / let us")

Restricted to **1st-person** subjects (I, we). When you see `ga-`,
translate as "let me" (singular) or "let us" (plural). Since the subject
is implicit, you usually don't see a separate 1st-person agent marker —
any `n` or `b` in the chain is now the **object**, not the subject.

Always **perfective**. Vowel-harmonized to whatever follows:

- `ga-mu-un-du₃` "let me build it"
- `gu-mu-...` (vowel-harmonized to `u`)
- `gi₄-bi₂-...` (vowel-harmonized to `i`)

To negate `ga-`, use `ga-ra-` (which combines `ga-` with `nu-` semantics
— "let me not...").

### 2.3 `ḫa-` — precative ("may he / let him")

Expresses an assertion, wish, or command. Translate as "may he/she/it"
or "let him/her/it."

Vowel-harmonizes:

- `ḫa-ba-` (before `ba-`)
- `ḫe₂-` (before something with `i` or `e`)
- `ḫu-mu-` (before `mu-`)

### 2.4 `ba-ra-` — categorical negation ("absolutely not")

A stronger version of `nu-`. Always written `ba-ra-` (never harmonized).
Useful as the precative's opposite: where `ḫa-` is "may it happen,"
`ba-ra-` is "may it never happen."

>

> 2. `nu-mu-un-du₃`

> 4. `ba-ra-mu-un-dug₄`

> 4. "He absolutely did not speak."

## §3 — Compound verbs revisited

You met compound verbs in lesson 2 (`inim–gi₄`, `gu₃–de₂`, `igi–bar`).
This lesson extends them in two ways.

### 3.1 The noun part takes suffixes

Because the noun is still a noun, it can carry possessives:

- `igi-ŋu₁₀ in-ši-bar` — "I looked at her" (igi "eye" + 1st-sg
  possessive)
- `igi-zu in-ši-bar` — "you looked at her"
- `igi-ni in-ši-in-bar` — "she looked at him" (`-ni` from `-ani` with the
  `a` dropped, because `igi` ends in `i`)
- `igi-bi in-ši-ib₂-bar` — "they looked at her" / "its eye looked at her"

You can also invent your own compound verbs when translating English
into Sumerian.

### 3.2 Oblique objects on compound verbs

The "object" of a compound verb is sometimes called an **oblique
object**. It's marked in one of three ways:

| Marker | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Locative-terminative `-e` | `ŋeštin-e šu ba-an-ti` | "he received wine" |
| Locative `-a` | `er₂-ra šu ba-an-ti` | "she received tears" |
| No marker at all | `dub šu ba-an-ti` | "she received the tablet" |

(`šu–ti` is "to receive" — literally "hand-take.")

### 3.3 Auxiliary verbs — `ak` ("to do") and `du₁₁` ("to say")

Sometimes the compound verb is fronted and an auxiliary verb (`ak` or
`du₁₁`) appears **in** the verbal chain instead. Same meaning, different
syntax.

- `e₂ ki us₂ mu-ak-e` — "she will firmly establish the house."

(Literally: "the house, foundation, she-does-it." The verb `ak`
substitutes for the underlying compound.)

## §4 — Imperatives

When you want to give a command, Sumerian moves the **verb to the front
of the chain**, often with an `-a` suffix (yes — *the pesky `-a`* once
more), and possibly some short tail.

### 4.1 Bare imperative

- `šum₂` "to give" → imperative `šum₂-ma` "Give!"
- `ŋen` "to go" → imperative `ŋen-na` "Go!"

This is structurally the same as the participle/passive `-a` we've seen,
but spoken with a commanding tone.

### 4.2 Imperative with retained chain

If the verb originally had a chain, the chain is preserved but the verb
moves to the front:

- `mu-šum₂` "you gave it" (declarative, 2nd singular)
- `šum₂-ma-ab` "Give it!" (imperative — verb fronted, `-a` from the
  preceding `mu-` shifted onto the verb, `-b` is the 3rd-non-human object
  "it")

**Diagnostic:** if you see `VERB + vowel + maybe more`, especially with
the verb at the **front** of what looks like a verbal chain, suspect
imperative.

## §5 — Non-finite forms

A **non-finite** verb form is one that doesn't carry tense / person /
number. We've already seen one: the perfective participle / passive in
`-a` (lesson 2's "pesky `-a`").

This lesson adds the **active imperfective participle** in `-e(d)`,
which translates to "the one who…" or "in order to…"

### 5.1 The `-e(d)` participle

The `d` is "ghostly" — present when a vowel follows, absent at word-end.
Patterns:

| Surface form | Normalization | Example translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gi₄-gi₄` | `gi₄.gi₄.e(d)` | "the one who returns" (no `-e` because base ends in vowel) |
| `šum₂-mu` | `šum₂.e(d)` | "the one who gives" |
| `tar-re-de₃` | `tar.ede` | "the one who decides" / "in order to decide" |
| `tar-re-da` | `tar.ede` | same as above |
| `tar-re-dam` | `tar.edam` | "the one who decides, it is" (with copula `-am`) |

### 5.2 Two-step translation heuristic

When you see one of these, try in this order:

1. **"The one who …"** — active participle.
2. **"In order to …"** — purpose clause.

Worked example: `en nam tar-re-de₃` →

1. First try: "the lord, the one who decides fates."
2. If that doesn't fit: "the lord, in order to decide fate…"

Another: `e₂ du₃-de₃` = `e₂ du₃.ede` →

1. First try: "the one who builds the house."
2. If that doesn't fit: "in order to build the house."

## §6 — Subordinate clauses

A subordinate clause is a sentence nested inside a sentence. English
example: *"The king, the one who built the temple, stood."*

Sumerian's most common subordination construction is **`lu₂ ... -a`**.
The `lu₂` ("man, one, someone") opens the clause; the `-a` closes it.
Everything in between is the subordinate clause.

Worked example:

> Base sentence: `lugal i₃-gub` — "the king stood."
>
> Subordinated: `lugal lu₂ e₂ mu-un-du₃-a i₃-gub`
>
> "The king, the one who built the temple, stood."

Read it as two separate sentences:

1. `lugal i₃-gub` — "the king stood."
2. `lu₂ e₂ mu-un-du₃-a` — "(he) the-one-who built the temple."

Then merge: "the king, the one who built the temple, stood."

> **Drill 6.1.** Break apart and translate:
> `lugal lu₂ uru-še₃ i₃-ŋen-a i₃-gub`
>
> <details><summary>Answer</summary>
>
> Subordinate clause: `lu₂ uru-še₃ i₃-ŋen-a` — "the one who went to the
> city."
>
> Main clause: `lugal ... i₃-gub` — "the king stood."
>
> Merged: "The king, the one who went to the city, stood."
>
> </details>

## §8 — End-of-lesson exercise

### Part A — Normalize and translate

1. nin dumu-še₃ lugal-la mu-ši-in-tud
2. lu₂ ŋⁱᵉˢ-eren-da uru-a i₃-ŋen  *(`ŋⁱᵉˢ` is a wood determinative; ignore
   it when looking up `eren` "cedar")*
3. lu₂-e e₂-a še mu-un-ŋar
4. piriŋ eden-ta ba-ra-ŋen  *(treat the `-ra-` as the ablative case marker
   inheriting an `a` from `ba-`; normalize as `-ta-`)*
5. ᵈutu-ra an-še₃ šu-zu-ne-ne ba-e-zig₃-ge-en-ze₂-en  *(subject is in the
   chain; ᵈutu is NOT the subject)*
6. piriŋ-e udu ib₂-gu₇
7. kaskal-la ŋeštin i₃-im-de₂-en-de₃-en  *(`im` here is `mu-` with a
   vowel-inheritance trick — normalize as `mu-`)*
8. ᵈinanna-e šu-ni-še₃ me-me ib₂-la₂-e
9. ga-am₃-du₃
10. lugal-ra dug₄-ga-ab  *(the leading `ga` is the vocalic prefix `a-`,
    not the cohortative — vowel harmony.)*

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `nin.e dumu.še₃ lugal.ak mu.ši.n.tud` — "The lady gave birth to the
   son of the king." Genitive on `lugal` (silent k), `-ši-` agrees with
   `-še₃`, `mu-` is ventive (giving birth = motion-toward), `n` is
   3rd-sg-human agent.
2. `lu₂.0 ŋⁱᵉˢ.eren.da uru.a i₃.ŋen` — "The man went into the city with
   cedar wood." Intransitive (absolutive 0); `-da` "with," `-a` locative
   "into."
3. `lu₂.e e₂.a še.0 mu.n.ŋar` — "The man set grain in the house."
   Transitive perfective.
4. `piriŋ.0 eden.ta ba.ta.ŋen` — "The lion went out from the steppe."
   Intransitive; `ba-` for non-human subject; ablative `-ta-` in the
   chain.
5. `ᵈutu.ra an.še₃ šu.zu.ene ba.e.zig₃.enzen` — "You (pl) raised your
   hands toward heaven to Utu." Note the subject is `-enzen` inside the
   chain. `šu.zu.ene` = "your hands" (plural via `-ene`).
6. `piriŋ.e udu.0 i₃.b.gu₇` — "The lion ate the sheep." Transitive
   perfective; non-human `b`.
7. `kaskal.a ŋeštin.0 i₃.mu.de₂.enden` — "We will pour out wine on the
   road." Imperfective transitive 1st-plural (object inside chain).
8. `ᵈinanna.e šu.ani.še₃ me.me.0 i₃.b.la₂.e` — "Inanna hangs the *mes*
   on her hand." Imperfective form #1 (`-e`); object `me-me` reduplicated
   for plural; `-ši-`/`-še₃` agree.
9. `ga.mu.du₃` — "Let me build it." Cohortative; `mu-` lost its `u` to
   `ga-`'s `a`.
10. `lugal.ra dug₄.a.b` — "(You) Say it to the king!" Imperative — verb
    fronted, vocalic prefix `a-`, non-human object `b`.

</details>

### Part B — Transliterate, normalize, translate

1. `𒈗 𒇽 𒂍𒂠 𒉌𒁺𒀀 𒉌𒁺`  *(subordinate clause — note the order of
   things)*
2. `𒂼𒈬 𒆠𒁉𒈾𒉌 𒄷𒉭𒉭 𒅔𒊑`  *(the superscript `𒄷` is a bird
   determinative)*
3. `𒀖𒂊 𒌨𒁇𒊏𒁕 𒄠 𒌈𒋺`

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `lugal lu₂ e₂-še₃ i₃-ŋen-a i₃-gub` → `lugal.0 lu₂ e₂.še₃ i₃.ŋen.a
   i₃.gub` — "The king, who went to the temple, stood." Subordinate
   clause bounded by `lu₂ ... -a`. Both verbs are intransitive
   perfective.
2. `ama-ŋu₁₀ ŋešbun₂-na-ni ᵐᵘˢᵉⁿ-nunuz-nunuz in-de₅` →
   `ama.ŋu₁₀.e ŋešbun₂.ani ᵐᵘˢᵉⁿnunuz.nunuz.0 i₃.n.de₅` —
   "My mother collected bird eggs for her meal."
   `ŋešbun₂-na-ni` = `ŋešbun₂.ani` "her meal"; `nunuz-nunuz` =
   plural eggs; `mušen` is the bird determinative; `-n-` is the
   3rd-sg-human agent.
3. `ab₂-e ur-bar-ra-da am ib₂-taka₄` → `ab₂.e urbara.da am.0
   i₃.b.taka₄` — "The cow left the bull behind with the wolf." Cow
   (ergative `-e`), wolf (comitative `-da`), bull (absolutive 0), `b`
   for non-human agent.

</details>

## §10 — Teaching notes for the agent

- This is a wide lesson — six topics. **Pace them out.** If the learner
  is fading, stop after compound verbs (§3) and resume modal prefixes
  the next session.
- The `-a` and `-e(d)` participles are easy to confuse. Drill at least
  three of each.
- Imperatives are easy to spot once you say "the verb is at the FRONT of
  the chain." Many learners try to parse imperatives as declaratives and
  get lost — alert them to the diagnostic.
- For `ga-` and `ḫa-`, **vowel harmony** is the trickiest part. SVC and
  `eme-gir-epsd2.find_verb_form` will show real attested forms; lean on
  them.
- The `lu₂ ... -a` subordination is the cleanest of several
  subordination constructions Sumerian has. The author of the source
  material is explicit: this is the only one taught at 101 level. Don't
  let yourself try to teach `dub-sar-bi-da` constructions or `-ne-še₃`
  purpose clauses here — that's 201 material.
- After Part A, ask the learner to compose **one** sentence of their own
  using a modal prefix and a case ending. Use `cuneify` to render it
  back as glyphs so they can see what they wrote.
- Close with the §9 farewell. The lesson series is meant to feel
  *complete* — the learner has a foundation, even if Sumerian itself
  isn't done with them.
